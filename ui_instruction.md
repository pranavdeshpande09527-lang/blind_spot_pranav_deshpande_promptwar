Design a minimalist single-page web UI for “THE BLIND SPOT”, an AI-powered decision blind-spot detection tool inspired by the visual language of a classic Indian newspaper.

The interface should help users examine their assumptions, identify overlooked factors, detect conflicts in their reasoning, and explore deeper questions before making a decision. The system must support critical thinking without making the decision for the user.

Strict Design Rules

- No logos, images, illustrations, or decorative icons.
- Use only text, typography, lines, borders, and layout.
- No rounded corners, pills, circular UI elements, soft cards, gradients, glassmorphism, or floating shadows.
- Every component must have sharp 90-degree corners.
- Avoid the appearance of a typical SaaS dashboard.
- Maintain a compact, structured, editorial newspaper feel.
- Avoid excessive whitespace while preserving readability.

Typography

- Use “Passion One Designed by Fontstage” for the “THE BLIND SPOT” masthead.
- Use “Libre Franklin” for all other text.
- Use strong typographic hierarchy, uppercase section labels, bold headlines, compact metadata, and newspaper-style editorial spacing.

Color Palette

- Warm off-white or paper-colored background.
- Near-black typography.
- Thin black rules and subtle grey dividers.
- Use a restrained muted red accent only for warnings, contradictions, or high-priority blind spots.
- Maintain high contrast and excellent readability.

Core User Workflow

Input Section

Create a prominent editorial input area where users can describe a decision they are considering. Include structured fields such as:

- Decision or question
- Context and background
- Options being considered
- Reasons supporting the current preference
- Constraints, priorities, and concerns
- People or factors affected by the decision
- Optional deadline or time horizon

Include a clear action such as “EXAMINE DECISION” or “FIND BLIND SPOTS”.

Stage 1: Decision Summary

Display the user’s input as a concise, neutral summary. Show:

- The decision being considered
- Available options
- Stated goals
- Key reasons
- Explicit constraints
- Important people or areas affected

The summary should reflect the user’s thinking accurately without judging or recommending an option.

Stage 2: Assumption Analysis

Use AI to identify assumptions present in the user’s reasoning. Classify them into categories such as:

- Unstated assumptions
- Assumptions that may be uncertain
- Assumptions requiring evidence
- Emotional or social influences
- Short-term versus long-term assumptions
- Possible confirmation bias

Each assumption should include a short explanation and a question that helps the user examine it.

Stage 3: Blind-Spot Detection

Present factors the user may have overlooked. Organize them in a newspaper-style editorial layout using sections such as:

- WHAT MAY BE MISSING
- WHO ELSE IS AFFECTED
- LONG-TERM IMPACT
- PRACTICAL RISKS
- EMOTIONAL FACTORS
- ALTERNATIVE PERSPECTIVES
- TRADE-OFFS NOT YET CONSIDERED

For every potential blind spot, show:

- The overlooked factor
- Why it may matter
- Which part of the user’s reasoning it relates to
- A reflective question for deeper consideration
- A priority level such as low, medium, or high

Stage 4: Reasoning Conflicts

Identify possible conflicts or tensions within the user’s reasoning. Examples may include:

- A stated goal conflicting with a preferred option
- Short-term benefits conflicting with long-term objectives
- Personal values conflicting with practical constraints
- Benefits being emphasized while risks are minimized
- One option being evaluated using different standards than another

Present these neutrally and avoid declaring the user’s reasoning wrong.

Stage 5: Perspective Expansion

Show alternative ways to look at the decision. Include perspectives such as:

- A future version of the user
- A person directly affected by the decision
- A cautious risk-focused perspective
- An optimistic opportunity-focused perspective
- A neutral outside observer

Each perspective should generate concise questions rather than advice or a final recommendation.

Final Reflection

Create a final structured reflection section containing:

- The three most important blind spots to examine
- The strongest assumptions behind the current preference
- The most important unanswered questions
- Information or evidence the user may want to gather
- A comparison of what changes if each major assumption is true or false
- A final “THINK BEFORE YOU DECIDE” checklist

Do not provide a final decision, recommendation, score, or winner. The interface should clearly communicate that THE BLIND SPOT helps users think more critically, but the decision remains theirs.

Visual Layout

Use a classic newspaper-inspired composition with:

- A large masthead at the top
- Thin horizontal rules separating sections
- Dense editorial columns
- Structured rectangular panels
- Compact data rows
- Uppercase labels
- Strong black headlines
- Red accent markers for warnings or important blind spots
- Clear progression from the user’s decision to assumptions, overlooked factors, conflicts, and reflection

The final design should feel like a serious editorial intelligence tool: thoughtful, sharp, trustworthy, text-focused, and distinctly different from a conventional AI dashboard.
